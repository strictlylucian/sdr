SDR — Sistem de Design Românesc, the Romanian Design System — proposes a shared language for designing and developing Romania’s digital public services. Its purpose is to help institutions build services faster, reduce repeated work and offer citizens a consistent experience.

## Why the project exists

There are substantial differences between Romania and several Western European countries in the maturity of digital public services and the organisation of shared design and development resources. France’s [Système de Design de l’État](https://www.systeme-de-design.gouv.fr/) brings together rules and reusable components for public-sector interfaces. In the Netherlands, [NL Design System](https://nldesignsystem.nl/) connects institutions, user research and shared solutions for accessible services.

Romania also has a substantial gap to close in the use of digital public services. [Eurostat’s figures for 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20260312-1) show that 24.1% of people aged 16–74 used public authorities’ websites or apps, compared with 71.9% across the European Union. Several factors contribute to this gap; a shared design language can contribute to services that are easier to build and use.

Without a common foundation, teams repeatedly solve the same problems: forms, navigation, error messages, information presentation and accessibility. Decisions are revisited across projects, while citizens encounter different ways to complete the same task. SDR aims to reduce this fragmentation through clear conventions, reusable components and shared documentation.

## What a shared system would offer

- **Faster development:** teams start with existing components and patterns, leaving more time for the service’s specific needs.
- **More consistent services:** familiar actions, fields and messages behave consistently across institutions.
- **Accessibility from the outset:** requirements are part of design, implementation and verification, supported by examples and documented limitations.
- **Reusable improvements:** fixes and solutions validated in one project can be adopted by other teams.
- **Easier collaboration:** designers, developers and content specialists work with the same vocabulary and conventions.

## A language suited to Romania

SDR seeks a recognisable Romanian identity through colour, typography, natural Romanian content and restrained use of cultural elements. Component behaviour and accessibility requirements draw on international standards and conventions familiar to users.

The library provides React components and an HTML edition with two files, CSS and JavaScript. Romanian and English documentation supports collaboration between local and international teams. Institutions can supply their own emblems and names within the same system of interactions.

## From proposal to use

SDR is an independent proposal, published as an experimental alpha. The next step is validation in real services through pilots, user research and collaboration with institutions, designers, developers and accessibility specialists.
