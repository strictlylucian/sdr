This study concerns an **individual’s application for a utility-work clearance** from Bucharest’s municipal public-lighting operator, CMIPB. The clearance checks proposed connection or network work against the public-lighting infrastructure; this is not a general contact request.

## Check for an existing digital service

Checked on **27 October 2026**: the operator’s [clearance service and document catalogue](https://www.cmipb.ro/page/197) publishes downloadable, signed application forms and specifies submission at its counter or by email. Its general contact form is described as a channel for reporting lighting faults, not a structured clearance application.

No dedicated transactional web application for this request was found in those published service routes or the official-service searches. **Email delivery of a completed dossier already exists.** The proposed change is structured application entry and document handling, not a claim that the operator has no electronic communication. This check is limited to publicly listed routes; the operator would need to confirm any unlisted or forthcoming system before a pilot.

## What the published form asks for

The one-page PDF asks for the applicant’s name, phone, email, address, representative and project, followed by a document checklist and signature. The linked source is specifically for individuals; the catalogue separately publishes company forms. Its PDF has selectable text but **no interactive form fields**.

## Why a digital journey is useful

- **A downloaded sheet still needs completion outside the service.** Reading the PDF online does not provide keyboard-operable fields, contextual checks or an answer review. The document must be completed and delivered as a dossier.
- **Essential requirements are separated from the answer spaces.** Identity, authority to act and project information are followed by a long list of drawings and technical documents. A structured journey can connect each required item with its upload and explain when representation documents apply.
- **The fixed page does not reflow around larger text or a phone screen.** Long blank lines and a dense document list remain a sheet-shaped interface. A responsive composition can make the same questions readable without shrinking the page.

The source is a recent revision; the problem is its document-based interaction, not an assumption that its date or underlying engineering requirements are obsolete.

## What SDR proposes

Three panels cover the applicant, the work and supporting documents. The beneficiary/representative choice reveals the relevant representative details and files. Required documents remain in the page, with individual file controls. PDF/JPG/JPEG/PNG and 5 MB per file are **prototype limits**, not verified CMIPB upload rules. No file contents are read, no dossier is delivered and no clearance or payment is issued.

## Mapping to the original

| Published form | SDR proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Applicant, contact and address | Labelled native fields in the first panel. |
| Representation and work description | Explicit capacity choice and conditional representative details. |
| Identity, urban-planning/connection approval, plans, memorandum and network solution | Separate, visible document controls matching the source checklist. Representation documents appear when relevant. |
| Signature and processing statement | Demonstration acknowledgement only; it is not an electronic signature or institutional consent. |

## What a pilot could establish

CMIPB would need to confirm that no existing application should be reused, then validate document rules, representation, signatures, secure storage, fees, technical review and status tracking. Research could examine incomplete dossiers and requests for missing documents; this prototype does not demonstrate a faster approval process.
