A public institution should be recognisable as part of the state without needing a completely different visual language from its neighbours. This study develops the original boards into a **family of identities**: a shared eagle, the institutional name and a colour indicating its institutional family.

The identities and colours below are study proposals, not identities adopted by the institutions shown.

## From separate logos to a shared identity

The British government identity system, introduced in 2012, replaced disparate approaches with a common construction: symbol, name and departmental family colour. Agencies inherit the colour of their parent department. The [2012 guidelines](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7dd9bce5274a5eb14e7912/hmg_identity_system_guidelines_2012.pdf) describe this mechanism. [Design102 explains the reasons for unification](https://design102.blog.gov.uk/2021/01/29/branding-government-how-can-good-design-help-build-public-trust/): recognition, consistency and lower costs when organisations change.

The Romanian proposal applies colour to the **whole visual signature**, rather than a side stripe. The eagle and construction stay consistent. Repetition builds recognition; the name and colour provide differentiation.

## Shared construction

- **One symbol.** The eagle uses the [Government-logo vector drawing on Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Guvernul_Rom%C3%A2niei_(2016-).svg), adapted to monochrome, with the same geometry for every institution.
- **One typography system.** Inter, with sentence-case names arranged over two or three lines. Names remain live text, rather than images.
- **Fixed proportions.** The eagle matches the height of the text block. The space between them is one quarter of that height: H/4. Longer names can use three lines within the same overall height.
- **Inherited colour.** The Ministry of Finance and ANAF share the same green. A change of name or organisation does not require a new symbol.

The full name identifies the institution in monochrome as well. Colour supplements that information. Symbol and text scale together, without distortion or reducing the name to initials. Marks retain Romanian names; English captions identify each institution.

## The identity family

The proposal uses a restrained palette of dark colours. Every colour has at least 7:1 contrast against white and works both on white and reversed. Blue and burgundy continue the directions explored in the original boards.

DNSC is coordinated by the prime minister and appears in the central-administration family. SRI retains a separate colour: parliamentary oversight does not make it a ministry or a government agency. The Ministry of Justice colour does not automatically extend to courts and prosecutors.

## In a service and a document

The signature can be prominent in a service header and quieter in a document. The finance family stays recognisable in ANAF services, while the same construction works in a Ministry of Justice letterhead.

Institutional colours belong to identity. Buttons, links, focus and errors retain their shared SDR meanings. A burgundy institutional identity does not turn every action into a red button.

## The original boards

### Balance · Blue

The first board presents a white-on-blue version and its blue-on-white counterpart. The main composition uses “Guvernul României” (Government of Romania). ## Where this study sits in SDR

The study is separate from the library packages. [Header](/en/components/header/) and [Page](/en/components/page/) continue to accept an application-provided emblem. None of these identities becomes a default.

Romania already has [Government-logo rules for EU-funded project communications](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocumentAfis/276473). This study explores a possible shared direction without changing those rules. Further development would need final vector heraldic artwork, print proofs and recognition testing at small sizes, including monochrome.
