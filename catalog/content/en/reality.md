Public-service interfaces have serious, recurring problems: inaccessible controls, unreadable guidance, inconsistent layouts and applications that still depend on paper. These examples need a complete overhaul, from the questions and page structure to the way people submit a request.

SDR shows what that overhaul can look like through real services, their current forms and working visual redesigns.

## Serious barriers, not cosmetic flaws

- **People cannot reliably identify fields with assistive technology.** The IGI and Gruiu forms have missing or incorrect label associations.
- **Essential information is difficult to read.** Small text, weak contrast and cramped labels make important instructions easy to miss.
- **Every institution presents the same questions differently.** Citizens must relearn layouts, controls and terminology for each service.
- **Downloading a document leaves the manual process intact.** Printing, signing, scanning and re-entering information add work for citizens and staff. Digital services need structured questions, document handling and submission.

A complete overhaul needs accessible controls, readable content, clear steps and a shared design language. The studies below demonstrate these changes with SDR components.

## Explore the redesigns

Each study contains the original, a short diagnosis and the SDR remake. All steps and answer review are visible in the documentation; the standalone version provides a step-by-step walkthrough. These are visual studies: nothing is submitted, and validation is deliberately omitted.

A pilot connects the redesign to institutional processes, submission and tracking, then tests it with citizens and assistive technology. Each study ends with the service-specific questions to resolve.
